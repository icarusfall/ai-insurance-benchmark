Play it yourself

Run a small insurer for twelve quarters. Price three lines of AI-liability business, set aside money for claims you have not heard about yet, buy reinsurance, rule on a claim each quarter and decide what to pay your shareholders. A house actuary plays the same world, with the same luck, alongside you.

This is a simplified cousin of the benchmark, with its own rules and numbers, and kinder. The language models in the real thing price fifty clients a quarter from their dossiers, for forty quarters, with no bail-out.

How to play

Goal

Beat the hurdle

Shareholders want 2% a quarter. Your score, R, is their wealth at the end against that path: 1.0 means you earned exactly what they asked. Cash sitting in the company earns only 1%.

Pricing

Cheap wins, then hurts

Undercut the going rate and you win more clients, but the extra ones are the riskier ones. Overprice and only the desperate buy.

The catch

Nobody knows the true risk

Each line may be riskier or safer than the whole market assumes. The market learns slowly. Can you learn faster?

The long tail

Claims arrive late

Only 40% of a quarter's claims are reported straight away; the rest trickle in over the next three quarters. Reserve for them, or the auditor will do it for you.

Kindness

One bail-out, some hindsight

If you go under once, the board injects $15m, at a cost to your score; the second time is final. Each quarter's report also tells you what your new policies will eventually cost. The real game never does.

world

Your decisions this quarter

Reserving: the money set aside for claims that have happened but are not yet reported. Assume too little and your profits look wonderful until the auditor checks. At the going rate a line costs about 60% of its premium in claims, if the market has the risk right.
